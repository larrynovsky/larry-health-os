# The Objectives of the [[CBCR Course]]
CBCR focuses on integrating knowledge from pathophysiology, epidemiology, and clinical decision-making to solve clinical problems systematically.
Students who complete the course can evaluate collected data, use biomedical and epidemiological knowledge in patient care, make a focused differential diagnosis, and provide suitable guidance. Additionally, they develop leadership skills for meetings on clinical reasoning.

## What Is [[Clinical Reasoning]] and Decision-Making?
Clinical decision-making involves rational considerations from the moment a patient presents at the doctor’s office until an end is reached in this contact. This process often entails solving medical questions or problems with quality arguments that include both pathophysiological and non-pathophysiological considerations.

## Rooms, Arrangement and Facilities
Suitable rooms for CBCR sessions feature square table placements with 2-4 chairs per side and a blackboard or flip-over chart. A consultant should be present to guide the session effectively.

## Overview
Developing a modern curriculum including CBCR requires careful planning and faculty development.

## Early Thinking of Clinical Reasoning: The Computer Analogy
Early models compared clinical reasoning to computer algorithms, assuming they would outperform human clinicians. However, computers struggle with non-verbal information critical for communication in healthcare. Content knowledge remains crucial but does not guarantee success in clinical reasoning.

## [[Mini-Lecture]] Tips
Mini-lectures should be concise and structured, focusing on directly applicable or enhancing understanding of topics. Maintain contact with the group to ensure message clarity and adapt to student preparation levels.

## Concepts and Definitions
Clinical reasoning is both a process and an outcome involving cognitive operations for observing, collecting, analyzing information, and making decisions that consider patient circumstances and preferences.
