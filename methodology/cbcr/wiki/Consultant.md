The **consultant** plays a critical role in [[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR) education as an overseer and facilitator of CBCR sessions. Their primary responsibilities include overseeing the session, assessing student participation, providing feedback to students and peer teachers, distributing written materials for subsequent sessions, and completing evaluation forms.

### Role Description
The consultant guides the session adaptively based on the quality of guidance provided by peer teachers, stepping in only when necessary to clarify or offer perspectives. They are not the primary source of information but rather support peer teachers in leading discussions. In many CBCR courses, consultants keep attendance records for each session and provide regular updates to the course director.

### Session Management
During sessions, consultants assess student preparation and active participation. Over time, they develop a balanced view of individual students' contributions, which informs their evaluation methods and interventions when needed. After each session, new peer teachers receive the next case materials from the consultant, ensuring that every student fulfills at least twice the role of peer teacher over the course.

### Continuous Improvement
Consultants contribute to continuous quality assurance by writing feedback about cases directly after sessions. This includes noting linguistic errors, medical inadequacies, and pedagogical improvements to be shared with the CBCR course director for ongoing refinement of the program. Course development often involves detailed planning to ensure that consultants can effectively guide sessions even if they are not specialists in all areas covered by cases.

### Materials and Resources
The written materials provided include a student version, a peer teacher version (with additional hints and hand-out texts), and a consultant version (which includes background information and model answers). All students receive the student case documents at the start of the course. The consultant ensures that upcoming sessions' peer teacher versions are distributed to prepare them for their roles in leading discussions.

### Logistics
Sessions typically last between 1.45-2.30 hours, with groups consisting of 10–13 students each. Scheduling logistics ensure minimal information exchange among groups to maintain case freshness and prevent premature knowledge dissemination.

### Roles During Sessions
Consultants help peer teachers manage their roles by ensuring active student engagement, managing time effectively, and summarizing key points. They also facilitate the use of materials like whiteboards or PowerPoint presentations for better group interaction and learning outcomes.

### Summary Role
At session ends, one student summarizes the case chronologically to train students in efficient case summary techniques essential for clinical practice. This activity emphasizes the importance of clear communication skills during CBCR sessions.
