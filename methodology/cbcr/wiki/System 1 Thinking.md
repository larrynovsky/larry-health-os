title: System 1 Thinking
updated: '2026-05-13'
---

Dual process theory argues that human thinking involves two processes: System 1 (fast, subconscious) and System 2 (slow, conscious). System 1 thinking, also known as nonanalytic reasoning or pattern recognition, is quick and effortless. In medicine, an example of System 1 thinking is immediately recognizing the cardinal features of Graves' disease in a patient presenting with exophthalmia, fine resting tremor, and thyromegaly ch03_08_system-1-and-2-thinking-as-dual-processes. This type of fast thinking is crucial for efficient clinical work as it prevents overloading working memory. However, System 1 thinking can be flawed, leading to errors or biases such as anchoring bias or premature closure wikilinks. Efficient clinical reasoning often requires a combination of both fast and slow thinking processes ch03_08_system-1-and-2-thinking-as-dual-processes.
