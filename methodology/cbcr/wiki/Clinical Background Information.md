Mini-lectures are crucial in case-based clinical reasoning education, providing students with necessary background information directly applicable to their cases or enhancing understanding of complex topics such as pathophysiology and diagnostic tests ch03_01_concepts-and-definitions. Tips for delivering effective mini-lectures include:

**Form:**
- Use minimal text on slides
- Incorporate images
- Maintain a clear structure

**Content:**
- Ensure the message is evident and has a clear conclusion
- Avoid excessive detail

**Presentation Skills:**
- Engage with students, checking understanding and asking questions
- Be mindful of voice and presentation style
- Adapt to varying levels of student preparation

Mini-lectures should not exceed 5 minutes in length and are designed to teach rather than recite content ch10_08_the-mini-lecture. This approach supports the development of clinical reasoning skills, which involve cognitive operations for observing, collecting, analyzing information, and making context-specific decisions. Clinical reasoning is essential for a physician’s performance and must consider patient perspectives and system environments ch03_01_concepts-and-definitions.

Understanding how doctors think and acquire clinical reasoning over time involves concepts from various fields such as Klahr and Dunbar's model of scientific discovery, which helps in understanding problem-solving in new medical contexts. This model highlights the expansion of a learner’s domain space through inquiry learning to better match expert knowledge ch03_01_concepts-and-definitions.
