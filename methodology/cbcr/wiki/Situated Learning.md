## Situated Learning

**Situated learning** is a concept that emphasizes the importance of context-specific factors in the process of clinical reasoning, particularly how physicians engage with patients and their environment to arrive at diagnoses. This contrasts sharply with traditional views that medical problem-solving involves generalizable skills alone.

In Elstein et al.'s seminal work on medical problem-solving ch03_01_concepts-and-definitions, researchers observed that a physician's performance on one case does not predict success in another, leading to the phenomenon known as **case specificity**. Similarly, **context specificity** highlights that physicians may diagnose different conditions for patients presenting with identical symptoms and histories, depending on their interaction within varying contexts ch03_09_case-specificity-and-context-specificity.

The idea of situativity encompasses this complexity by considering clinical reasoning as a dynamic process involving the physician (their knowledge and experience), the patient, the environment, and system factors such as clinic staffing and support systems ch03_12_bias-and-error-in-clinical-reasoning. Within this framework, **situated learning** focuses on how participation in activities shapes identity formation and understanding within a specific setting. This approach underscores that clinical reasoning is not merely the application of learned facts but arises from interactions between these contextual elements.

By recognizing the role of situativity and situated learning in medical education, educators can better design curriculum to enhance learners' ability to reason effectively in real-world scenarios ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development. This includes understanding how different contexts influence reasoning outcomes and developing strategies that account for these nuances ch10_02_the-objectives-of-the-cbcr-course.
