The curriculum development process for medical education, as originally described by Kern and colleagues from Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine and revised by Thomas et al. (2016), recommends following the **Kern six steps** to ensure a structured approach:

1. **Problem identification and general needs assessment**: Determine why change is necessary and identify societal health problems that should be prioritized in a new curriculum.

2. **Needs assessment of targeted learners**: Understand the motivations and backgrounds of students to tailor curricula effectively.

3. **Goals and objectives**: Define specific, measurable learner objectives that are behaviorally formulated to monitor student progress.

4. **Educational strategies**: Choose suitable teaching methods based on educational theory to achieve the defined learning goals.

5. **Implementation**: Identify resources, gain administrative support, and anticipate barriers before implementing changes through a pilot program.

6. **Evaluation and feedback**: Collect data from users, measure outcomes, analyze results, and use feedback to continuously improve curriculum quality.

This process is crucial for developing an effective medical education curriculum that meets societal needs and enhances student learning experiences. [[ch09_01_overview]] provides a brief introduction to the principles involved in curriculum development.
