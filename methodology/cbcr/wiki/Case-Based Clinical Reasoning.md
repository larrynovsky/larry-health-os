### Summary of the CBCR Method
The Case-based [[Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR) method is an educational approach aimed at preclinical medical students, designed to enhance their ability to handle clinical problems. Unlike Problem-based Learning (PBL), which focuses on personal educational objectives and knowledge acquisition, CBCR emphasizes applying systematically acquired prior knowledge in a clinical context.

### What Is Clinical Reasoning?
Clinical reasoning involves the mental processes clinicians use when encountering patients to diagnose conditions and recommend treatments. It is often categorized into two systems: System 1 (rapid pattern recognition) and System 2 (analytical, deliberate thinking).

### [[Illness Script]]
An illness script is a comprehensive representation in a clinician's mind of an illness, including its causes, symptoms, expected diagnostic findings, and management options.
illness-script

### [[Faculty Development]] for CBCR
Faculty development for a CBCR course includes written instructions, training case writers and consultants, and instruction of students. This ensures that faculty members are well-prepared to guide students through the process of clinical reasoning.

#### Case Study: Tbilisi State Medical University
Introducing CBCR at Tbilisi State Medical University involved several steps, including a workshop on CBCR rationale and concepts in 2012, training in case writing and demonstration of CBCR by Utrecht medical students, piloting sessions for third-year students, formal inclusion in the curriculum, and spreading to other universities. Feedback indicated that students valued CBCR as an excellent teaching tool.

### Alignment with Actual Cases Discussed
CBCR courses aim to install basic frameworks of illness scripts for junior students. The Utrecht CBCR test closely follows cases discussed during sessions, reinforcing learned scripts through rehearsal and testing.

### Selecting Themes for CBCR Cases
Cases should focus on important medical problems representing illness scripts that all students need to understand before clinical clerkships. They should include educational value, represent various clinical domains, and develop a network of related disease patterns in the student’s mind.
selecting-themes-for-cbcr-cases

### Rooms, Arrangement and Facilities
Suitable rooms for CBCR sessions allow students to face each other with their peer teachers. A recommended layout includes square tables with 2–4 chairs on each side. Consultants sit at a corner to emphasize their role as facilitators.
rooms-arrangement-and-facilities

### Course Development for CBCR
Introducing CBCR can be part of curriculum modernization without major organizational changes. Steps include assessing educational needs, content needs, defining learner characteristics, general course framework, assessment methods, coordination and teachers, funding, and program evaluation.
course-development-for-cbcr

### Indications for the Effectiveness of CBCR Method
While there are no direct controlled studies comparing CBCR to other approaches, indirect evidence suggests its effectiveness. Components like a clearly formulated problem and focused group discussion have shown positive impacts on student learning.
indications-for-the-effectiveness-of-the-cbcr-method

### Student Role in CBCR Sessions
Students must read about the topic in advance and prepare initial questions for cases. Active verbal participation is essential to develop clinical reasoning skills, even if thoughts become routine over time.
student-role

CBCR can serve as a model approach to ignite curriculum modernization by promoting integration, being student-centered and problem-based, thereby encouraging broader application of these principles in medical education.
