# Script Concordance Test
The **script concordance test** (SCT) is a method designed to assess clinical reasoning skills, particularly useful for preclinical students who have not yet encountered real patients. This assessment format was introduced by Charlin et al. in 2000 and has since been widely adopted but also criticized for its validity and practicality.
### Structure
The SCT presents a short patient vignette followed by the introduction of a new finding or piece of information. The candidate must then score how this finding influences the likelihood of a diagnostic hypothesis, rating it on a scale from -2 (much less likely) to +2 (much more likely), with 0 indicating no change.
### Scoring
Model answers are constructed using a panel of experts who answer these questions. Since expert opinions may vary, a weighting is applied based on the number of experts choosing each value. This method allows for the creation of a scored test that can be objectively evaluated.
### Criticisms and Limitations
While widely used, SCTs face criticism regarding their validity and practicality (van den Broek et al., 2012; Lineberry et al., 2013). The variability in expert opinions introduces an element of subjectivity into the scoring process. Additionally, constructing a reliable test with SCT requires careful item writing to ensure construct validity.
### Comparison with Other Formats
In comparison to other question types such as **modified essay or short-answer questions** (Rademakers et al., 2005), and **clinical reasoning problems** (Groves et al., 2002), the SCT has unique features. For instance, modified essays require hand scoring, which can be time-consuming for large cohorts of students.
### Application in CBCR Courses
For [[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR) courses with numerous participants, a written or preferably electronic test format is recommended to ensure reliable examinations. The combination of SCT and other question formats allows for comprehensive assessment tailored to preclinical education needs (Chapter 7).
