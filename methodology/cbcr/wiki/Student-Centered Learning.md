## Student-Centered Learning

CBCR ([[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]]) has been used as an approach to ignite curriculum modernization since 2005 in countries like Moldova, Georgia, Ukraine, and Azerbaijan ch01_05_cbcr-as-an-approach-to-ignite-curriculum-modernization. This method is particularly useful in medical education contexts where curricula are heavily lecture-based. CBCR can be integrated within an existing curriculum without causing major disruptions, while also exemplifying recommended modern educational practices ch02_04_william_osler_and_the_differential_diagnosis.

One key feature of CBCR is its highly student-centered and problem-based approach. This encourages active learning and the integration of knowledge across different subjects or topics, which enhances students' ability to apply their understanding in practical scenarios ch01_03_essential_features_of_cbcr_education. Schools observing CBCR can consider how these principles can be applied more broadly throughout preclinical courses.

The method fosters a learning environment where students are encouraged to take an active role in their education, promoting critical thinking and problem-solving skills. By engaging with real-world clinical cases, students develop a deeper understanding of medical concepts and enhance their diagnostic reasoning abilities ch08_05_consultant_version.

This volume provides a detailed description that enables schools to pilot CBCR as a means to modernize preclinical curricula, emphasizing its potential impact on student learning outcomes and the overall educational experience.
