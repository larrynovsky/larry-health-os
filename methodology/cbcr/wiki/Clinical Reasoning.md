title: Clinical Reasoning
updated: '2026-05-13'
---

Clinical reasoning is the cognitive process through which healthcare professionals make diagnostic and therapeutic decisions based on patient data. It encompasses both System 1 (fast, intuitive) and System 2 (slow, analytical) thinking as dual processes [[ch03_08_system 1 and 2 thinking as dual processes]].

### Encapsulation of Knowledge and the [[Intermediate Effect]]
The intermediate effect refers to how less experienced clinicians sometimes outperform more seasoned professionals in recalling specific details from clinical cases due to their use of pathophysiological reasoning rather than encapsulated diagnostic labels or simplified causal models ch03_07_encapsulation-of-knowledge-and-the-intermediate-effect.

### Neuroscience and [[Visual Expertise]]
Clinical reasoning involves rapid pattern recognition facilitated by visual expertise, which activates various regions of the prefrontal cortex as observed in functional MRI studies ch03_13_neuroscience-and-visual-expertise-in-clinical-reasoning.

### [[Bias]] and Error
Errors in clinical reasoning often stem from cognitive biases such as availability bias, representative bias, confirmation bias, anchoring bias, bounded rationality bias, and outcome bias. While these biases may serve as heuristics for successful behavior, they can also lead to diagnostic errors ch03_12_bias-and-error-in-clinical-reasoning.

### [[Illness Script]] Theory
Illness scripts are high-level conceptual knowledge structures in long-term memory representing general event sequences. Experienced clinicians activate relevant illness scripts quickly upon seeing a patient, facilitating diagnosis and treatment planning ch03_06_illness-script-theory.

### Prototyping and [[Semantic Qualifiers]]
Semantic qualifiers like 'acute,' 'monoarticular,' and 'arthritis' help clinicians organize clinical information into meaningful categories that link to prototypes of diseases in their minds ch03_05_prototyping-and-semantic-qualifiers.

### [[Hypothesis-Driven Inquiry]]
This approach emphasizes the importance of forming hypotheses early in a patient encounter, asking purposeful questions, and recognizing clinically useful findings. It helps discriminate between diagnostic considerations during history taking and physical examination ch04_06_hypothesis-driven-inquiry.

### Current Methods for Assessing Clinical Reasoning
Assessment methods like script concordance tests, extended matching questions, and comprehensive integrative puzzle questions are used to evaluate clinical reasoning skills. However, these methods have limitations such as cueing effects or the inability to weigh items within answers ch05_02_current-methods-of-assessing-clinical-reasoning.

### [[CBCR Test]]
The Utrecht CBCR test is a written format designed specifically for preclinical students. It closely follows cases discussed in CBCR sessions, reinforcing learned illness scripts through rehearsal and testing without substantial transfer of learning ch07_02_alignment-with-actual-cases-discussed.

### Evidence of Effectiveness
CBCR courses have been well-received with high student ratings. While no direct comparative studies exist due to the unique implementation across cohorts, some evidence suggests superior clinical reasoning performance among students who completed these courses ch06_10_evidence-of-effectiveness.
