In the context of [[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR) education, managing cognitive load is critical for developing effective illness scripts. Illness scripts are mental representations of disease entities that combine **factors causing or preceding a disease**, **the actual pathology**, and **the effect of the pathology showing as signs, symptoms, and expected diagnostic findings** [[ch03_02_early thinking of clinical reasoning the computer analogy]]. Originally proposed with three elements—enabling conditions, fault, and consequences—the fourth element, course and management, is often added to reflect how clinicians quickly take a next step suggested by the script. This process relies on pattern recognition in working memory [[ch03_12_bias and error in clinical reasoning]], which has a limited capacity for processing information, typically no more than seven units or chunks of data at once (Miller 1956). To mitigate cognitive overload, CBCR aims to teach students to see illnesses as a unit of information by using simple, prototype cases that can be easily remembered ch03_04_knowledge-representations-to-support-reasoning. Illness scripts stored in long-term memory are activated and recalled instantly when a pattern recognition process occurs. This approach helps manage cognitive load by allowing students to handle prototypical patient problems efficiently without overloading their working memory.
