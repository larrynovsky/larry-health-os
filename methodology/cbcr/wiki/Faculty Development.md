Most faculty members in medical schools and universities have been trained to be adequate clinicians or scientists but few are trained specifically for teaching roles. As education evolves, the need for formal training in pedagogy becomes more apparent. Table 9.3 outlines a model of teaching certificates at UMC Utrecht that ranges from student teaching to advanced teaching and postgraduate scholarly educator certification. This framework is crucial as it provides detailed competencies necessary for medical educators in domains such as development, organization, execution, coaching, assessment, and program evaluation across different levels of responsibility.

**Faculty Development for CBCR** involves four main components: written instructions about the background and practicalities of cases-based clinical reasoning education; training of case writers to draft, discuss, and refine cases collaboratively; training of consultants through observing actual sessions or via instructional videos demonstrating the student perspective; and training students in their roles as peer teachers. This development ensures that both faculty members and students are adequately prepared for CBCR.

A **case study** from Tbilisi State Medical University (TSMU) in Georgia exemplifies the introduction of CBCR within a modernization project funded by the EU-Tempus program (2011-2014). Key steps included introducing the rationale and concepts, training faculty members in case writing and CBCR demonstration, piloting sessions with positive feedback leading to its formal inclusion as a compulsory course. Success was marked by improved student engagement and clinical thinking skills.

Challenges were identified during implementation such as overly active consultants and less engaged students, highlighting the need for continuous teacher training to foster an effective learning environment.
