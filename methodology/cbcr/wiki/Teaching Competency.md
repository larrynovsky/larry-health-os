## Teaching Competency
Medical educators must be trained before they are allowed to teach, similar to how students cannot treat patients without proper training ch09_05_the-aim-of-faculty-development. This is reflected in the increasing requirements for teaching certificates and diplomas as seen at UMC Utrecht with their model of teaching certificates (Table 9.3) ch09_05_the-aim-of-faculty-development.

### Domains and Levels of Responsibility
Molenaar et al. provide an elaborate framework that distinguishes domains such as development, organization, execution, coaching, assessment, and program evaluation for medical educators ch09_05_the-aim-of-faculty-development. The levels of responsibility are categorized into leadership, coordination, and actual teaching (macro-meso-micro) ch09_05_the-aim-of-faculty-development, highlighting the need for detailed competencies in faculty development.

## Evidence of Effectiveness
CBCR courses have been well-received across decades with high student ratings. A comparative study by Schmidt et al. (1996) found superior performance of an Amsterdam cohort compared to other universities, possibly attributed to their CBCR course ch06_10_evidence-of-effectiveness. Additionally, recent studies indicate that preclinical students develop better illness scripts for cases discussed in the CBCR course than common cases not covered in it (Keemink et al., n.d.) ch06_10_evidence-of-effectiveness.

Teaching competencies and evidence of effectiveness underscore the importance of faculty development in ensuring high-quality education and training.
