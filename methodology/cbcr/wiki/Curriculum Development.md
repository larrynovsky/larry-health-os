# The Process of Curriculum Development

The curriculum development process in medical education follows a structured approach, originally well described by Kern and colleagues from Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development. This framework has been revised by Thomas et al. (2016) into the following six steps:

1. **Problem identification and general needs assessment**: Determine why change is necessary and identify societal health problems that should be prioritized in a new curriculum.
2. **Needs assessment of targeted learners**: Engage students to understand their motivations and learning preferences, ensuring they feel motivated to invest effort into the curriculum.
3. **Goals and objectives**: Define specific and measurable learner objectives that are behaviorally formulated to monitor progress effectively.
4. **Educational strategies**: Choose appropriate teaching methods based on defined objectives.
5. **Implementation**: Identify resources needed; secure administrative support, communication strategy, and address barriers to change before full implementation.
6. **Evaluation and feedback**: Design evaluation procedures, collect data, analyze results, and feed findings back into the quality assurance process.

These steps are critical for developing a modern curriculum that includes [[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR) education [[ch09_01_overview]]. A full curriculum description should include:
- **Mission statement**: A concise rationale summarizing the curriculum's overall intention.
- **Objectives**: Learning goals reflecting societal needs at a national level.
- **Intended learners and admission policy**: Description of preferred student backgrounds and criteria for selection.
- **Educational philosophy**: Guiding principles grounded in educational theory.
- **Curriculum framework**: Visualization of the curriculum, often depicted using charts that show individual units arranged by weeks and years.
- **Individual units**: Detailed descriptions of each unit or course including objectives and teaching methods.
- **Assessment methods**: Various assessment approaches aligned with Miller’s pyramid ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development.
- **Governance, coordination, and administration**: Structured governance to ensure collaboration and quality control.
- **Funding and facilities**: Adequate funding for teaching time, support, and physical infrastructure such as classrooms and internet access.
- **Quality assurance and faculty development**: Continuous monitoring of the curriculum's effectiveness and modifications as needed. Teachers must be trained in effective student-centered education ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development.

Curricula are living entities that require careful planning, delivery, and reception by both teachers and students. This includes understanding the differences between planned, delivered, experienced, and hidden curricula [[ch02_06_artificial intelligence and problem based learning]].

Ultimately, a successful curriculum is heavily influenced by the context in which it operates and the intrinsic motivation of its learners.
