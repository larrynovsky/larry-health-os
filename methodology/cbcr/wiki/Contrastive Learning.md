# Contrastive Learning

Contrastive learning involves prompting learners to explicitly search for similarities and differences between problems [[ch03_08_system 1 and 2 thinking as dual processes]]. Ark et al. (2007) demonstrated that this approach leads to superior diagnostic accuracy compared to traditional serial learning methods when applied to the identification of key features in abnormal electrocardiograms (ECGs). By comparing and contrasting an initial ECG with a normal ECG and one typical of a plausible alternative diagnosis, learners are encouraged to identify critical distinguishing features.

Bordage (1994) recommended focusing on deep learning of prototypical clinical presentations related to a single problem representation in order to create strong memory anchors. Once students have developed schemas for clinical cases [[ch02_06_artificial intelligence and problem based learning]], teachers can introduce the concept of differential diagnosis.

For any given problem representation, at least two plausible diagnostic hypotheses are selected for comparison, with preselection being important at this stage to ensure realistic and easily distinguishable options. For example, in Alicia's case [[ch10_02_the objectives of the cbcr course]], comparing rheumatoid arthritis with osteoarthritis helps students learn distinguishing features.

Table 4.5 below contrasts competing illness scripts for rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis:

| Example of a problem representation | A middle-aged female with a chronic, gradually progressive symmetrical oligoarticular process involving small joints characterized by moderate to severe morning stiffness |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Exemplar diagnosis                   | 1 - Osteoarthritis | 2 - Rheumatoid arthritis |
| Enabling conditions                 | Age, sex, race; over 50 years; either sex             | 30-60 years, F:M ratio 3:1        |
| Family history, genetics            | +/− family history                                          | + family history; shared epitope, HLA-DRB1 |
| Habits, exposures, medications      | None                                                                 | Smoking                          |
| Nested comorbidities                | None                                                                 | Coronary artery disease           |
| Pathophysiological fault             | Mechanical, degenerative; cartilage breakdown and subsequent bone erosion of juxta-articular bone | Inflammatory, immunologic; synovitis, pannus and subsequent bone erosion  |
| Clinical consequences               | Onset: Gradual; Site: Small, large joints; appendicular; polyarticular; involves DIP             | Onset: Gradual; Site: Small, large joints; appendicular; polyarticular; usually spares DIP |
| Severity                             | Mild                                                                 | Moderate                         |
| Chronology                          | Chronic persistent                                             | Chronic persistent               |
| Exam findings                       | Boney enlargement of joint; mild tenderness if any            | Warmth; erythema; tenderness; swelling; occasional rheumatoid nodules |
| Laboratory findings                 | None                                                                 | Elevated ESR; rheumatoid factor anti-CCP    |
| Imaging findings                    | Sclerosis of bone under cartilage; joint space narrowing      | Erosive polyarthritis; joint space narrowing |

This method aids students in identifying distinguishing features and enhances their diagnostic reasoning skills [[ch06_02_scheduling groups]].
