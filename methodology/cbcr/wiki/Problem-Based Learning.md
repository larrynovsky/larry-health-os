Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to computer programs characterized by flexibility and adaptivity, accommodating broad user input with diverse responses. In medicine, AI systems like INTERNIST and MYCIN were developed to support automated diagnosis but did not fulfill the promise of teaching clinical reasoning directly ch02_06_artificial-intelligence-and-problem-based-learning. This approach was contrasted with problem-based learning (PBL), pioneered at McMaster University Medical School in Canada, which aimed to teach students to become effective problem solvers through a curriculum focused on solving real-world problems rather than accumulating knowledge independently of context ch01_04_indications-for-the-effectiveness-of-the-cbcr-method. PBL's effectiveness is debated; while some studies indicate its benefits, particularly in fostering critical thinking and collaborative learning, others suggest it does not significantly outperform traditional educational methods ch02_06_artificial-intelligence-and-problem-based-learning. The case-based clinical reasoning (CBCR) method builds on these principles by focusing on constructing illness scripts—mental representations of diseases—and developing diagnostic thinking habits through small group sessions guided by a clinical tutor, aiming to integrate basic science and clinical knowledge ch01_04_indications-for-the-effectiveness-of-the-cbcr-method.
