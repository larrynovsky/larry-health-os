Introducing [[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR) into an existing medical curriculum requires careful planning, particularly through a thorough educational needs assessment. This step ensures that the institution recognizes the necessity of CBCR, whether due to the desire for better-prepared clinical students, experimentation with modern curricula, or integration of basic science education with clinical thinking [[ch01_05_cbcr as an approach to ignite curriculum modernization]].

The needs assessment can be conducted through structured interviews or questionnaires among key stakeholders such as clinicians, basic scientists, and students. A clear report summarizing the findings helps secure support from decision-making bodies like committees or deans [[ch09_01_overview]].

After identifying the need, content-specific assessments determine which pathologies should be prioritized in CBCR cases and at what stage of the curriculum these cases should be introduced. This ensures that students have relevant prior knowledge to effectively engage with the material [[ch08_05_consultant version]].

The development process follows a structured framework including defining learners, creating a general course outline, setting assessment methods, coordinating teachers and consultants, securing funding, and establishing evaluation systems for continuous improvement [[ch10_02_the objectives of the cbcr course]] [[ch09_03_the process of curriculum development]].
