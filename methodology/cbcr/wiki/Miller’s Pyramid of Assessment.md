Miller's four-level pyramid of assessment provides a framework for evaluating clinical reasoning skills at different stages. The levels are **knows** (knowledge about facts), **knows how** (ability to apply knowledge in specific contexts), **shows how** (demonstration of skills through standardized scenarios), and **does** (real-world application). For preclinical education, the focus is on **knows how** and **shows how**, as these levels are more suitable for written or electronic tests. Such formats allow for efficient assessment of large groups of students and generation of reliable scores. A **modified essay/short-answer questions** format can be particularly effective due to its reliability and ease in testing key features of clinical reasoning ten Cate O., 1997. This aligns well with the principles of CBCR ([[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]]) education, which aims to assess students' ability to reason about patient cases before actual clinical exposure [[ch03_02_early thinking of clinical reasoning the computer analogy]]. Miller’s pyramid also guides the curriculum development process in medical education, influencing how learning goals and methods of assessment are structured. It is a cornerstone for ensuring that educational strategies effectively prepare learners for real-world scenarios ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development
