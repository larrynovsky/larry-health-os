A curriculum, defined as 'a planned educational experience' thomas_et_al_2016, has evolved to encompass multiple levels of education: macro (government requirements), meso (school and university plans), and micro (classroom guidance). Janet Grant expanded this definition by proposing that a curriculum is a statement of intended aims, objectives, content, experiences, outcomes, processes, training structure, methods of learning, teaching, feedback, supervision, and organizational management grant_2010. Mulder and ten Cate refined this further with a ten-element model for comprehensive curriculum description, including mission statements, learner descriptions, educational philosophy, general framework, unit/course descriptions, assessment methods, organizational structures, personnel conditions, finances, facilities, and quality assurance mulder_and_ten_cate_2006. These elements adhere to international medical curricula standards lindgren_2012. However, a curriculum's effectiveness hinges on its delivery by teachers and reception by students. Various types of curricula are distinguished: planned (as defined), delivered (by teachers), experienced (by students), and hidden (unspoken rules and behaviors). Awareness of these distinctions is crucial for ensuring that all pathways lead to effective learning towards common educational goals hafferty_and_franks_1994prideaux_2003. Individual student motivation can influence their adherence to or deviation from planned paths, provided such deviations foster constructive career development ten_cate_et_al_2011.
