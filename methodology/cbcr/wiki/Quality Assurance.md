Every curriculum must continuously be monitored for its quality and modified if needed. Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) is a well-known cycle that can establish the foundation for a curriculum quality assurance procedure ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development. PDCA involves planning improvements, executing them, checking their effectiveness, and acting on feedback to make further adjustments.

Quality assurance includes ensuring teachers are trained and qualified to teach, particularly when the education is not identical to their own education. Understanding the learning process of students is crucial for effective student-centered education ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development. Medical schools must provide time for teaching and reward high-quality teaching efforts.

The process involves specific goals and measurable objectives that help monitor student progress, along with educational strategies that lead to suitable methods of teaching. Implementation requires identifying resources, obtaining support, anticipating barriers to change, and piloting before full implementation. Evaluation and feedback include identifying users, resources, and issues; designing procedures and questions; choosing or constructing measurement instruments; collecting and analyzing data; and efficiently reporting results to feed into a new cycle of quality assurance ch09_03_the-process-of-curriculum-development.
