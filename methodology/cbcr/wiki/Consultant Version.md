The full consultant version of the CBCR case includes all information, suggestions, and hints for peer teachers and detailed answers to all questions necessary for non-expert clinicians. It also contains all patient information that peer teachers should disclose during the session.

CBCR ([[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]]) is a method designed at the Academic Medical Center of University of Amsterdam in 1992 [[ch01_03_essential features of cbcr education]]. The approach uses small group sessions over an extended period, typically every 3-4 weeks. These sessions are independent from concurrent courses and blocks to ensure that students apply previously acquired knowledge without specific cues or diagnoses.

Each CBCR session includes a case vignette reflecting how a patient presents in the clinic, followed by questions and assignments. The facilitator interrupts with new findings at appropriate moments. A full case covers the course of a problem from initial presentation to follow-up after treatment, focusing on key stages.

Sessions are led by peer teachers who take turns facilitating over the whole course. They receive additional information about the patient in advance, which they disclose during sessions according to given instructions. A clinician is present as a consultant when guidance is requested or helpful.

Study materials include:
- General study guide with rules and procedures
- Student version of written CBCR case material per session
- Peer teacher version with extra information and hints
- Full consultant version including all answers, suggestions, and patient information needed for facilitation by a clinician unfamiliar with the case or discipline.

Students are assessed at the end of the course on their knowledge of illnesses and active participation as both students and peer teachers [[ch01_05_cbcr as an approach to ignite curriculum modernization]].
