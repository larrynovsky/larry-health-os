Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) is an emerging method in neuroscience research aimed at exploring the biological underpinnings of clinical reasoning, particularly focusing on System 1 thinking. One notable study by Durning et al. utilized fMRI to investigate brain processes in novices and experts solving clinical problems through vignette-based multiple-choice questions ch03_02_early-thinking-of-clinical-reasoning. The research revealed activation across various regions of the prefrontal cortex, suggesting potential for further investigation into how these areas contribute to expert reasoning capabilities.

A related field is visual expertise in medicine. This encompasses not only disciplines such as radiology and pathology but also primary care settings where visual observation can lead to immediate System 1 recognition ch03_12_bias-and-error-in-clinical-reasoning. Understanding the neurological basis of this rapid recognition could enhance our comprehension of how medical professionals quickly diagnose conditions based on visual cues.

These methods, including fMRI and electroencephalogram (EEG), hold promise for deepening our understanding of clinical reasoning mechanisms. However, their application to preclinical education requires careful alignment with established approaches like CBCR ch01_03_essential-features-of-cbcr-education.

In summary, while current research provides valuable insights into the neural processes involved in clinical decision-making, there remains a need for further investigation to fully understand and effectively teach these skills.
