updated: '2026-05-13'

The **availability bias** in clinical reasoning refers to the tendency where a differential diagnosis is influenced by what can be easily recalled from memory rather than being based on prevalence or objective evidence (ch03_12_bias-and-error-in-clinical-reasoning). This creates a false sense of how common certain conditions are, potentially leading to incorrect diagnoses. The bias stems from the ease with which certain information comes to mind due to recent experiences or frequent encounters, rather than its true frequency in clinical practice.

In educational settings aimed at improving clinical reasoning skills through **[[Case-Based Clinical Reasoning]] (CBCR)** ([[ch01_03_essential features of cbcr education]]), learners are trained to recognize and mitigate such biases. This involves encouraging the consideration of a broader range of diagnoses, rather than relying solely on what is most readily remembered or has recently been encountered.

Further understanding of availability bias requires an examination of other cognitive biases in clinical reasoning (ch03_12_bias-and-error-in-clinical-reasoning), such as **representative bias** and **confirmation bias**, which together can lead to diagnostic errors. Addressing these biases is a key component of modern medical education, particularly within the context of developing curricula that incorporate CBCR ([[ch09_06_faculty development for cbcr]]).
