In the context of the Utrecht CBCR test, participation also contributes to a student's final score in addition to their performance on the actual test. The overall grading system assigns an 88% weight to the test score and a 12% weight to the participation score ch07_07_rules-and-regulations-around-the-utrecht-cbcr-test. This approach ensures that active engagement throughout the course, both as a student participant and as a peer teacher, is valued alongside academic performance.

Active participation encompasses various activities such as engaging in group discussions, performing well during case presentations, and fulfilling responsibilities associated with being a peer teacher. The specific criteria for calculating this participation score are detailed within the model study guide provided in Chapter 10 of the [Principles and Practice of Case-based [[Clinical Reasoning]] Education](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-64828-6).

Students who do not meet the course requirements can retake the examination to improve their scores ch07_07_rules-and-regulations-around-the-utrecht-cbcr-test. This system acknowledges the importance of both theoretical knowledge and practical engagement in clinical reasoning education.
