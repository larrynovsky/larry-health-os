### Introduction
Peer teaching is an educational method with theoretical foundations that enhances learning clinical reasoning through small group discussions led by students themselves. This approach ensures active participation from all members and provides a platform for just-in-time information sharing within the context of case-based clinical reasoning (CBCR) education.

### Benefits of Peer Teaching in CBCR
Peer teaching is beneficial because it stimulates knowledge acquisition in ways that studying for exams does not, often leading to deeper understanding. Social and cognitive congruence concepts explain why students benefit more from communicating with peers or near-peers rather than expert teachers. This setup facilitates a better mutual comprehension among learners.

### Role of [[Peer Teachers]]
Peer teachers are responsible for guiding group discussions by providing just-in-time information about clinical cases, such as diagnostic test results that may be necessary to order during the session. They must prepare in advance with additional details about each case and disclose these appropriately during sessions under facilitation guidelines provided beforehand.

### Practical Application
A CBCR course consists of regular small group meetings over extended periods, typically every 3-4 weeks but can vary based on institutional needs. Each session is led by two to three peer teachers who take turns throughout the academic year. Sessions focus on applying previously acquired knowledge without cues towards specific diagnoses.

### Summary
Peer teaching in CBCR education promotes active reasoning through oral communication and ensures that all participants engage actively, fostering a deeper understanding of clinical cases and enhancing diagnostic skills over time.
