An **illness script** (сценарий болезни) is a mental representation of disease entities combining three elements in a script: factors causing or preceding a disease, the actual pathology, and the effect of the pathology showing as signs, symptoms, and expected diagnostic findings. Some authors add a fourth element: course and management.

Illness scripts are stored as units in long-term memory that can be simultaneously activated when a pattern recognition process occurs based on patient information presented to a clinician. This process is usually not deliberately executed but occurs spontaneously due to the temporal nature of illness scripts, which enables clinicians to quickly take the next step suggested by the script.

The human working memory's limited capacity (Miller 1956) necessitates using labels to combine multiple pieces of information into one unit, such as an illness script for a condition like type II diabetes. This chunking approach aids in managing cognitive load during clinical reasoning and supports effective decision-making by facilitating quick recognition of common patterns.

In case-based clinical reasoning (CBCR) education, students are presented with complete patient scripts that include enabling conditions (often derived from history taking) to consequences (as presenting signs and symptoms). These scenarios help build illness script units in long-term memory. Simple prototype cases are used initially to enable students to easily remember the causes, symptoms, and management of common illnesses.

The development of an **illness script mental repository** allows students to integrate foundational science concepts with clinical findings from history taking, physical examination, and diagnostic testing (Custers 2015). This structured approach helps students consider features of a typical diagnosis and aids in the formation of illness scripts over time as they gain more direct or vicarious experience.

The CBCR method emphasizes active reasoning through oral communication and peer teaching, which encourages knowledge acquisition and reinforces the use of illness scripts. By engaging with whole but not overly complex cases early in their medical education, students can learn to recognize common patterns efficiently.

An illness script mental repository is crucial for effective clinical reasoning as it provides a framework for understanding disease presentations and aids in diagnosing patients accurately. This repository forms over time through repeated exposure to patient cases and reflection on these experiences.
